      content:
        -
          type: paragraph
          content:
            -
              type: text
              text: "Brandon Mill is located on the West side of the City of Greenville, SC. The primary mill building was built under the management of\_ J. Irving Westervelt and constructed by Captain Jacob W. Cage in 1901. It was designed in Early Twentieth-Century Commercial Style brick masonry and eventually added onto. The mill complex continued to expand with additions like the engine house, boiler house, machine shop, cloth building, cotton house, and fan house. Later the pump house, smoke stack, water tower, and ash hopper were added. The mill complex was surrounded by a mill village which housed many of the employees of the mill. Brandon Mill played a significant role in the development of the textile industry in the city of Greenville from 1901 to the 1950s. Because of the mill’s success, a strong community formed and supported economic and social development.\_"

                text: "After Brandon Mill was constructed and began hiring employees, the community around the mill quickly grew. Hundreds of houses were built surrounding the mill\_ creating a village. By the year 1918, the company built a baseball park, a school, two churches, a nursery, a library, and a nurse’s station. These additions helped create a lively and close knit network of families.\_"
          -
            type: paragraph
            content:
              -
                type: text
                text: "Through the tough years of the Great Depression, the mill president decided to reduce hours for the employees instead of eliminating jobs. This helped create a sense of loyalty to the mill and staff. Not long after, World War II broke out across the ocean and large amounts of textiles were needed. The mill prospered during the 1940s and the government consistently ordered duck cloth, medical gauze, and uniform twill fabric to help the war cause. Through the 1960s and 1970s the mill’s revenue slowly decreased and the mill closed in 1977.\_"

                text: 'Since the mills closing and the decline of the textile industry, thousands of jobs were lost and families were forced to move away. This had a major effect on the community and many businesses in the village were forced to close its doors along with the mill.'

Through time and hard work, the village is now full of determined small business owners and locals that want to celebrate the history of Brandon Mill's contribution to the West Greenville area."

              text: "After a local art museum suspended its art education program in 2013, the artists and teachers were looking for a new space to gather with students who wanted to continue to learn. The teachers conceived the idea of creating a space that held art classes, art studios, and a gallery to display the work of local artists. The goal was to “enrich the cultural fabric of our community through visual arts promotion, education, and inspiration.\"\_"

On May 1, 2015, GCCA opened its doors and held its first First Friday event.\_"
